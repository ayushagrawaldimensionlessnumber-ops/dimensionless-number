Froude Number Tutorial

The Froude number applies wherever a fluid has a free surface, such as rivers, spillways, ship hulls and tanks being filled. Two forces compete. Gravity pulls the fluid down and levels the surface. Inertia keeps the fluid moving. Fr compares them, and it also compares the flow speed with the speed of surface waves. Every animation below can be changed with the sliders.

Step 1 of 4

Gravity and inertia on a free surface

A raised bump on a free surface carries extra weight, so its higher pressure pushes water sideways, away from the crest. The water overshoots because of its inertia, and the disturbance runs along the surface as a wave. A flat surface has nothing to restore, so gravity only matters once the surface is displaced. For a layer of depth L the weight of fluid in play scales as Fg ~ ρgL³, and long waves travel at the speed c = √(gL). Stronger gravity or a deeper layer means faster waves. The water also flows at a speed U, and moving fluid carries inertia, Fi ~ ρU²L². The fluid element on the right shows both forces: weight pulls it down, and its momentum pushes it forward. Arrow lengths are to scale for each force, up to a cap.

Step 2 of 4

The ratio is the Froude number

The inertial force scale is Fi ~ ρU²L², as in the previous chapter. Dividing it by the weight scale gives the square of the Froude number, and the density cancels:

Fr² = Fi / Fg = ρU²L² / ρgL³ = U² / gL

The balance tips to the right when inertia wins (Fr above 1) and to the left when gravity wins (Fr below 1). The tilt follows log10(Fr).

Step 3 of 4

Flow speed against wave speed

Since c = √(gL), the Froude number is also Fr = U / c, the flow speed divided by the speed of surface waves. A fixed disturbance in a stream sends out ripples at speed c while the current carries them downstream at speed U. Whether ripples can climb upstream depends on Fr.

Step 4 of 4

The hydraulic jump

Fast, shallow flow with Fr above 1 cannot slow down smoothly, because no wave can travel upstream to warn it. It slows abruptly in a turbulent roller called a hydraulic jump, and the flow leaves deeper and slower with Fr below 1. For a rectangular channel the depth ratio is y₂/y₁ = ½(√(1+8Fr₁²) − 1). You can see a jump where a tap's stream hits a sink.

Rules of thumb

FrFlow typeWhat it means
< 1Subcritical (tranquil)Gravity wins. Waves travel upstream, so downstream conditions such as a weir or a bend affect the flow upstream. Rivers are usually in this range.
= 1CriticalFlow speed equals wave speed. Waves stand still. This is the minimum-energy condition and occurs at the crest of a weir.
> 1Supercritical (rapid)Inertia wins. Waves are swept downstream, and a hydraulic jump forms where the flow slows back to subcritical.

Froude scaling: a small model of a ship or a spillway behaves like the full-size one when the two have the same Fr, because the ratio of inertia to gravity is then the same. The model speed is Um = Up√(Lm/Lp).
